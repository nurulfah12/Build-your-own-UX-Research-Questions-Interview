Research in 6 easy lessons. Like a science project.

Before you design an app, you find out what people really need. That is research. These six lessons show you how, in plain words. Each lesson has an example, a rule to remember, and a short quiz.

Our example for every lesson

Your school wants an app that shows students if the canteen queue is long. You need to do research first.

Words explained

Interview
A talk with one person, with questions you prepared.
Survey
The same questions sent to many people.
Qualitative
Stories. It tells you why or how.
Quantitative
Numbers. It tells you how many or how often.
Baseline
The “before” number, taken before your app exists.
Prototype
An early test version of your app. It does not need to work for real.
Usability test
You watch someone try a task on your prototype.
Observed vs self-reported
Observed: you saw it. Self-reported: they told you. Seeing is stronger, because people forget.

Cheat sheet

  1. Aim: “I want to find out ___ so I can decide ___.”
  2. Parts: 3 to 5 short labels, not questions.
  3. Way: talk first, then count.
  4. Ask: past, fair, one thing, no mention of your idea.
  5. Fair: check every question, then test it on one friend.
  6. Proof: same task before and after. Say how you got each number.
